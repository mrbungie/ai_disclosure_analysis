# MBA in International Business Program — XXXVI Edition (September 2025 – September 2026)

## Final Project: Writing Guide and Rules

*Date: April 2026*

---

## Nature and Goals of the Final Dissertation

The final dissertation is aimed at demonstrating the knowledge, skills and techniques acquired by the MBA candidate over the program's duration.

It has thus to be considered as an individual work, carried out in autonomy by the candidate, and not a work that is carried out together with a faculty member.

In the definition, planning and preparation steps of the dissertation, the main interface for the candidate is the Program Director.

---

## Topic and Initial Guidance

The topic of the dissertation may deal with arguments studied during the course, or issues related to a planned career or past experience.

Topics shall have a managerial/business relevance and must be, ideally, addressed from two perspectives: general and empirical.

Participants are required to identify and submit the topic proposal by mid-May 2026 so to receive initial feedback and ensure that the subject selected fulfils the aim of the Final Dissertation.

The topic must be approved by Prof. Tracogna and Prof. Bortoluzzi and approached according to depth and quality expected at an MBA level.

---

## Supervision

Participants will not be assigned a supervisor but are allowed to contact the School's faculty for advice and guidance during the development of the thesis. This may include bibliographical advice, exchange of ideas or suggestions on research. Any inadequacies identified while reviewing the thesis can be pointed out but not corrected by the faculty.

---

## Structure of the Final Dissertation

- The final dissertation must be written in English.
- The length of the dissertation must be a minimum of 30 and a maximum of 60 pages, up to 150,000 characters, excluding the attachments/appendix.
- Graphs and figures will be allowed if complementary to the text. They should be in English and must not exceed 20 pages.
- A bibliography of material consulted as well as the sources of data and enclosed graphs or tables must be included.

The dissertation will include the following compulsory parts:

- Executive summary
- Introduction
- Contents, organized in chapters and paragraphs (and subparagraphs if needed)
- Conclusions

**Executive summary:** 1–2 pages suggested. The aim is to briefly summarize the contents of the whole dissertation as if you were to submit your work to a business executive interested in quickly understanding which are the main contents, topics and inquiry-fields of the dissertation.

**Introduction:** 2–3 pages suggested. The aim is to introduce the main topic of the dissertation and the importance of the topic. Further, you shall also explain the methodology of the dissertation work, the key driving questions and the expected outcomes. Please note that the Introduction is not a summary of the dissertation but is meant to explain the context in which the dissertation's contents are placed.

**Chapters, paragraphs and sub-paragraphs:** it is suggested to use numbers, such as Chapter 1, paragraph 1.1 – 1.2 etc., subparagraph 1.1.1 – 1.1 etc.

**Conclusions:** 1–2 pages suggested. The aim is to present the main outcomes of the work and derive general indications and suggestions for managers and businesses.

## Bibliography and References

A list of references should follow the alphabetical order.

- **For articles** (Journals, Reviews, or other publications not in a book format): Surname and name of the author, year of publication, title, name of the Journal/Review, page/pages.
  > Example: Porter, M. E. (2000). Location, competition and economic development: Local clusters in a global economy. *Economic development quarterly*, 14(1), 15-34.

- **For books:** Surname and name of the author, year of publication, title, publisher.
  > Example: Porter, M. E. (2011). *Competitive advantage of nations: creating and sustaining superior performance.* Simon and Schuster.

- **For websites:** main address of the website.
  > Example (avoid "www" or "http"): bankingworld.com

- **For non-published materials** (such as internal materials, brochures, presentations and other sources of information): Surname and name of the author or Institution, title, type of document, year.
  > Example for a marketing research carried out by external consultants or companies: Nielsen, "Consumer trends in the Italian financial market", Allianz internal document, 2013
  >
  > Example for a set of slides presented by Dr. Marco Rossi at a conference: Rossi, M., "The future of online banking", presentation held at the conference "Big data and the banking industry", Milan 2013

## Antiplagiarism: Quotations & Sources

The outcome of your work MUST make clear what your contribution to the paper is, and what is instead the contribution of other authors. In order to achieve this result, you must use quotations and mention the sources of information.

The paper will be scanned against plagiarism with specific software that scans billions of webpages, articles, books and private databases to match the content of the paper submitted with these sources.

In order to avoid any plagiarism issue, you MUST follow these rules:

- **QUOTATIONS:** use "…." when copy-pasting content, and also mention the source (the same source will be included in the bibliography).
  > Ex: John Black underlined that "insurance is about buying risks" (Black, J., 2013)

- **REFERENCES:** if a particular position and/or content is sourced from a particular document or from the web, even if not completely copy-pasted, please mention the source within the text of the paper and not only in the bibliography!
  > Ex: There are two views on which one was the first insurance company founded in Europe. The first one […bla.. bla] (Black, J., 2013). The second one […bla.. bla…] (White, M., 2012)
  >
  > Ex: The following paragraph is mainly based on the work of White M. (2012) and Black J. (2013).

Turnitin, the antiplagiarism software, besides underlining the copy-pasted materials and its sources, will also return the following indexes:

- **The plagiarism index:** this is the % of the paper that is not properly quoted (thus copy-pasted without quotation marks ".."): this MUST be lower than 5% to be acceptable. If the plagiarism index is higher than 10% the paper will be rejected and sent back to the candidate.

- **The copy-paste index:** the total amount of content (in %) that had been copy-pasted (even if properly quoted): this must be lower than 10% (thus, at least 90% of the content must be written by yourself, in original form). If the "copy-paste index" is higher than 10% the paper will be rejected and sent back to the candidate.

- **The single source index:** this is the % of copy-pasted materials (properly quoted or not) from each one of the original sources identified by Turnitin. A single source (such as a particular book, or paper, or website) cannot be used for sourcing more than 1% of the paper (whether the content is properly quoted between "…" or not). As a rule, if the "single source index" is higher than 1%, the paper will be rejected and sent back to the candidate unless there is a specific reason for such a high index.

The previous indexes are used only as a minimum technical requirement for accepting the paper and submitting it to the Discussant and the Commission's members for the discussion. Please consider that the Discussant and the Commission's expectation is to receive a paper with around 0% plagiarism index and with a copy-paste index that is much lower than 10%. The Discussant and the Commission will keep into consideration the impact of a high plagiarism index and/or of a high copy-paste index when grading it.

## Grade & Weight of the Final Dissertation

The final dissertation is worth 4 credits on a total of 60 credits.

The grade of the final dissertation will keep into consideration both the written dissertation and its oral presentation in front of the Commission.

The final dissertation grade will be based on the following criteria:

- The managerial and business relevance of the topic at stake;
- The clear definition of the paper's objectives and methodologies and its expected outcomes;
- The adequate use — in framing the topic — of theories, models, concepts derived from the academic literature;
- The coherent development of the paper as set out in the introduction;
- The richness and relevance of the conclusions;
- The quality of the oral presentation and discussion.
