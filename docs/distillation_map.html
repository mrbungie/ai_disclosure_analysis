Distilling an Expensive Classifier into Cheap Programs

A fixed codebook applied by an LLM serves as the reference classifier for AI-disclosure content, paired with a deliberately wider scope rule for retrieval. No external gold-standard labels exist for the construct, and running the reference over every paragraph of the corpus is out of reach, so it is applied to designed samples only.

The Meta-Harness outer loop is then used twice, in cascade, to distill it into programs cheap enough to run on everything: a high-recall prefilter, then a six-label classifier over what the prefilter admits. Their target is fidelity to the reference, and the inference they avoid is a reported result.

0 · Concept discovery (Phase 0)

Upstream of both cycles, and run once, before any fitting or estimation sample exists. Renamed layers: Phase 0 is Induction, Cycle 1 / Distillation 1 is Selection, Cycle 2 / Distillation 2 is Distillation.

1 · The cascade

The reference sits above both stages and is never run at corpus scale. Each distillation spends LLM inference on a sample and returns a program that runs on everything.

Reference classifiers — sampled use only Distillation 1 — prefilter Distillation 2 — classifier
Which errors are recoverable. Under the sampling design, seed-screen errors primarily affect efficiency rather than identification, provided every relevant stratum retains positive inclusion probability: the screen defines strata, not membership, so the reference can discover positives it missed. Errors in the reference are different in kind — distillation reproduces them, and a program at high fidelity to a poor reference is a faithful reproduction of a poor reference. That asymmetry is why diagnostic effort concentrates on the reference, before the budget is committed.

2 · The distillation procedure

Both stages share the same search/holdout split and optimization mechanics; their sampling strata differ. The strata drawn below are stage 1's, built from the seed screen over the corpus — lexical, plus an optional semantic hit from Phase 0's ConceptSeed (§0) once frozen — stage 2 works inside the candidate frame and needs its own design.

The stages share one implementation; what differs is passed in.

prefilter  = distill(population = corpus,
                     reference  = scope_rule,
                     programs   = keyword_sets,
                     objective  = min_volume_subject_to_recall,
                     sampler    = corpus_sampler)

candidates = prefilter(corpus)

classifier = distill(population = candidates,
                     reference  = codebook,
                     programs   = boolean_formulas_over_bow,
                     objective  = macro_balanced_accuracy,
                     sampler    = candidate_sampler)
Three constraints inside the loop. The evaluation budget and stopping rule are fixed before the search starts, so there is no looking at the holdout and deciding on one more round. The proposer inspects prior code, scores and raw traces as the paper designs it — that access is where its ablation puts most of the gain — but its output is confined to a closed grammar of keyword sets and boolean formulas, so freedom goes into the search rather than into the class of program that comes out. And proposed programs may not contain literal strings or identifiers lifted from specific search-sample documents; the trace access that makes the search work also makes memorization easy.

3 · The two instantiations

Same procedure, different arguments. Stage 2 cannot begin until stage 1 is frozen, because stage 1 creates the population stage 2 samples from.

Three methodological distinctions matter here. First, stage 1's objective is constrained rather than maximized: unconstrained recall has a trivial optimum — admit everything — so the search minimizes volume subject to a floor fixed in advance. Second, that floor is a search recall measured on a sample deliberately weighted toward hard cases, so it is not corpus recall; the corpus figure is estimated separately on the probability holdout and is the one reported. Third, the six dimensions are six independent binary decisions, not one sixty-four-class problem, and their base rates will differ enough that raw agreement can be trivially high by predicting the majority class. Balanced accuracy — the mean of sensitivity and specificity — is therefore the operational measure of fidelity for each binary label, fixed before the search rather than chosen after seeing which statistic flatters the result. Wherever this document says fidelity for stage 2, it means that quantity, not the share of matching decisions.

The two references have different scopes, on purpose. The relevance reference asks whether a passage discusses AI at all; the dimensions codebook defines the construct that lives inside that scope. Scope is deliberately the wider of the two, which is what makes a recall floor with an unoptimized precision coherent — admitting text that turns out to carry none of the six dimensions is the over-inclusion the design pays for. Those all-zero chunks are therefore not a contradiction between the two references and not evidence of drift. Their rate is still worth reporting, as the prefilter's effective precision measured against the construct rather than against scope: it says how much of the candidate frame is noise under the definition that matters.

6 · Reliability checks and post-hoc construct validation

Two different questions: whether the reference is stable enough to distill, and whether the resulting measure behaves like the construct it claims to capture.

Reliability — before spending

A small diagnostic sample, run before any large-scale judging, looking for gross failures: labels that flip with item order, outputs that contradict the codebook on their face, instability across repeats, a trivial rewording changing the answer, or a second model applying the same codebook disagreeing wholesale. This establishes stability, not correctness. Anything it finds is fixed in the codebook before distillation starts.

Construct validation — after the panel

Expected sector patterns, temporal evolution against known AI adoption, association with R&D intensity, AI patents and AI hiring, and robustness to alternative specifications. Strong departures from pre-specified external expectations would motivate scrutiny of the measure and its assumptions; they are not treated as ground truth in disguise, since the external proxies carry their own composition and strategy effects.

One rule that protects both. Scope rule and codebook are frozen for the duration of a run and pinned as one release — changing either mid-way changes the objective function and makes everything before the change incomparable to everything after. Improving them is a new run, not a continuation. The same applies downstream: if construct validation produces disappointing patterns and the codebook is revised in response, the validation stops being post-hoc and a fresh evaluation has to be separated out.

7 · Build order and open choices

Dependencies are strict at the start and loosen once the samples exist. Nothing below has been built; sample sizes stay open until corpus prevalence is measured.

  1. Phase 0 — draw a discovery sample and induce a ConceptSeed. Independent of the fitting/estimation samples used later. LLM induction plus embeddings over that sample propose concepts, semantic anchors, lexical candidates, and candidate rules (§0). Freeze it — a versioned, immutable artifact — before step 4 draws any stage-1 sample.
  2. Codebook v1. The operational definition, with resolved edge cases and a version number. Everything downstream measures agreement with this text.
  3. Reliability check on both references. Small sample, repeats and perturbations. The codebook carries the construct and needs the closer look; the scope rule errs inward by design. Fix them here or not at all.
  4. Seed screen over the whole corpus. Deliberately over-inclusive, recording hit counts per paragraph and per filing — lexical hits from the keyword list, plus semantic hits from the frozen ConceptSeed if Phase 0 is enabled. Its accuracy is not assumed; it only defines strata.
  5. Draw stage-1 samples. Search: weighted toward weak hits and toward no-hit paragraphs in filings that hit elsewhere. Holdout: probabilistic, stratified by year and sector, inclusion probability recorded per unit, with guaranteed coverage of all three screen strata including filings with no hits at all.
  6. Apply the stage's frozen reference to the sampled units.
  7. Run stage 1 under its fixed budget, select on the declared metric, open the holdout once.
  8. Deploy the prefilter over the corpus to produce the candidate frame, then repeat steps 5–7 for stage 2 on that frame, with the six binary labels.
  9. Build the panel, then validate the construct against external firm characteristics.
Open choices: the value of the stage-1 recall floor r₀; how much judging budget the cold-filing stratum receives, which sets how tight the corpus recall bound can be; whether the codebook reference emits the six labels in one pass or six; whether to fall back to a deterministic search rule if the agentic proposer proves hard to reproduce across runs; and, for Phase 0, which embedding model is active (configs/config.json: phase0.active_embedding_model — swappable, and every model's cache is kept once computed, so trying several costs disk, not lost work) and where the semantic-hit threshold should sit.
Phase 0's freeze rule is the same rule as §6's, extended upstream. A ConceptSeed is frozen before step 4 draws any fitting/estimation sample, exactly as the codebook and scope rule are frozen before large-scale distillation. Producing a new ConceptSeed version after that point — because a downstream run surfaced a miss and it was tempting to patch an anchor — is a new Phase 0 run, not an edit to this one; the version that was actually frozen when sampling began is what stays comparable to what got measured against it.

Before any fitting sample is drawn, Phase 0 — Induction — reads a small, independent discovery sample of the corpus with an LLM plus local sentence embeddings and proposes a versioned ConceptSeed: named concepts, positive/negative semantic anchors, candidate lexical terms, and candidate rules, frozen before it can see what a later stage fails to catch. Because exhaustive LLM-based coding is prohibitively expensive, a versioned codebook applied by an LLM defines the construct, and together with a deliberately wider scope rule forms two costly reference classifiers — paragraph-level scope and chunk-level dimensions. The Meta-Harness outer-loop procedure is then used twice, sequentially — Cycle 1 / Distillation 1 is Selection, Cycle 2 / Distillation 2 is Distillation — to distill it into inexpensive executable programs. The seed screen that stage 1 samples from is a sampling aid rather than a source of truth, lexical plus, once Phase 0 is frozen, semantic, so under the probability-sampling design its errors primarily affect sampling efficiency rather than identification; errors in the reference do not have that property, and it is therefore frozen and checked for reliability before large-scale distillation. The first program retrieves in-scope paragraphs from the full corpus under a recall floor and a volume objective; the second reproduces the codebook's six binary labels within those candidates, with text that carries none of them being the over-inclusion the wider scope deliberately buys. Each is optimized on a reusable search sample and evaluated once on an independent probability holdout. Reported performance is fidelity to the reference rather than ground-truth accuracy, alongside the reduction in expensive inference the distillation achieves. The resulting corpus-level measures are then subjected to post-hoc construct validation.
